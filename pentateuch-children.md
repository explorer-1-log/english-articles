<p><br /></p><p><i>Editor's Note: Following its initial publication and an earlier update regarding Exodus 23:28, this article was further revised to refine the textual comparisons between the Masoretic Text (MT) and the Samaritan Pentateuch (SP) in Numbers 21:3 and Numbers 21:24, as well as related cross-references in Deuteronomy.&nbsp;</i><i>Please see the note at the end of the article for the SP source reference.</i></p><p>In academic discussions and modern critiques of the Hebrew Bible, passages depicting the slaughter of children during warfare (often associated with the concept of herem, or total destruction) are frequently cited as direct, verbatim commands from YHWH. However, when the scope of inquiry is strictly confined to the literal text of the Pentateuch (Genesis through Deuteronomy)—setting aside later historical or prophetic books as well as non-warfare divine judgments—a very different picture emerges. By examining the exact grammatical speaker ("The Mechanics of Voice") alongside textual variants between the Jewish Masoretic Text (MT) and the Israelite Samaritan Pentateuch (SP), this study demonstrates that YHWH never issues a direct, first-person command to kill or smite children in warfare.</p>
<p><br /></p><h2 style="text-align: left;">1. Methodological Framework: The Mechanics of Voice</h2><p></p>
<p>To separate verbatim divine decrees from human narrative framing within the Pentateuch, texts must be categorized by grammatical voice and attribution:</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;">• Direct First-Person Divine Speech</span></b>: Passages introduced by "YHWH spoke to Moses, saying..." where YHWH speaks directly in the first-person singular ("I will...") regarding the terms of the covenant (e.g., Exodus 23, Exodus 34, Leviticus 26).</p><p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;">• Mediated Third-Person Mosaic Speech</span></b>: Passages where Moses acts as the narrator or orator, speaking in his own first-person voice ("I command you") while referring to YHWH in the third person ("YHWH your God"). As explicitly noted in Deuteronomy 5:4-5 and 5:22-27, direct divine address to the assembly of Israel ceased after the Sinai event, leaving Moses as the sole interpretive mediator.</p><p><br /></p><h2 style="text-align: left;">2. Direct First-Person Speech of YHWH (Exodus 23 and 34)</h2>
<p>When YHWH speaks directly in the first person regarding the conquest of Canaan, the mechanism is divine expulsion, not human-executed extermination of children. Both the Masoretic Text (MT) and the Samaritan Pentateuch (SP) are entirely consistent on this point.</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: large;">• Exodus 23:20-33 (Divine Expulsion via Hornets/Wasps)</span></b>:</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;"> • YHWH's Action</span></b>: YHWH speaks in the first person, stating, "I will blot them out" (v. 23) and "I will send hornets/wasps" (Hebrew: ha-tsir'ah, v. 28) to "drive out" (Hebrew: garash) the Hivites, Canaanites, and Hittites. <b><span style="font-size: medium;">While the Masoretic Text (MT) names only these three groups in verse 28, the Samaritan Pentateuch (SP) preserves a fuller parallel reading that lists all seven nations: the Kaanannee, Ehmarree, Ihttee, Girgeshee, Ferizzee, Ibbee, and Yeboosee</span></b>. Furthermore, YHWH explicitly favors gradual expulsion ("Little by little I will drive them out from before you," v. 30) over sudden annihilation.</p><p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;"> • Israel's Mandate</span></b>: Israel's destructive command is strictly restricted to inanimate cultic objects: "utterly overthrow them and break their pillars in pieces" (v. 24). There is zero mention of slaughtering civilians or children.</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: large;">• Exodus 34:11-16 (Covenant Renewal)</span></b>:</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;"> • YHWH's Action</span></b>: YHWH states in the first person, "Behold, I am driving out before you..." (v. 11). The subject of the verb is YHWH, and the action is expulsion, not slaughter.</p><p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;"> • Israel's Mandate</span></b>: Israel is commanded solely to avoid treaties and destroy pagan altars, pillars, and Asherim (v. 13). Neither children nor non-combatants are targeted.</p>
<p><br /></p><h2 style="text-align: left;">3. Warfare Passages in the Book of Numbers (Textual and Speaker Analysis)</h2><p></p>
<p>There are only four warfare passages in the Book of Numbers connected to herem or the killing of children. None of them contain a first-person command from YHWH.</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: large;">• Numbers 21:2-3 (The Vow at Hormah)</span></b>:</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;"> • Speaker</span></b>: Israel ("And Israel vowed a vow to YHWH...").</p><p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;"> • Analysis</span></b>: The execution of herem (utter destruction) upon the Canaanite cities was a human-initiated conditional vow, not a divine decree originating from YHWH. <b><span style="font-size: medium;"><i>(Textual Notes: While largely parallel, SP explicitly adds the phrase "in his hand" after "delivered the Canaanite" in v. 3, whereas MT omits it).</i></span></b></p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: large;">• Numbers 21:24 (The Defeat of King Sihon)</span></b>:</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;"> • Analysis</span></b>: Records that Israel struck Sihon with the edge of the sword and possessed his land. While the term herem does not appear in either tradition, the texts diverge regarding the casualties: <b><i><span style="font-size: medium;">MT reads that Israel struck him alone and "he [Israel] possessed" his land, whereas SP explicitly expands the text to include "and his sons and all his people" and shifts the verb to plural ("and they possessed")—a direct inverse of the textual pattern seen in the defeat of King Og in v. 35.</span></i></b></p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: large;">• Numbers 21:35 (The Defeat of King Og of Bashan)</span></b>:</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;"> • Masoretic Text (MT)</span></b>: "So they smote him, and his sons, and all his people, until there was none left him alive..."</p><p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;"> • Samaritan Pentateuch (SP)</span></b>: "And they killed him, [ — ] and all his people, until there was no remnant left him. And they possessed his land."</p><p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;"> • Analysis</span></b>: The explicit reference to killing Og's "sons" is completely absent in the SP, indicating a likely scribal harmonization or expansion in the proto-Masoretic tradition.</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: large;">• Numbers 31:1-18 (The Midianite Campaign)</span></b>:</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;"> • YHWH's Speech (vv. 1-2)</span></b>: "And YHWH/Shehmaa spoke to Moses, saying, 'Avenge the people of Israel on the Midianites...'" There is no command to kill captives or children.</p><p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;"> • The Army's Action (vv. 7-9)</span></b>: Following ancient warfare conventions, the soldiers kill the adult males and take the women and little ones (Hebrew: taf) captive.</p><p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;"> • Moses' Speech (vv. 14-17)</span></b>: "And Moses was angry... And Moses said to them, '...Now therefore, kill every male among the little ones...'"</p><p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;"> • Analysis</span></b>: Preserved identically in both MT and SP, the order to execute the Midianite male children in v. 17 is issued exclusively on Moses' own spoken authority, completely separate from YHWH's verbatim instructions in vv. 1-2.</p>
<p><br /></p><h2 style="text-align: left;">4. Mediated Mosaic Speeches in Deuteronomy (Chapters 1-34)</h2><p></p>
<p>Every explicit command or historical claim linking herem and the killing of children to YHWH occurs exclusively within Deuteronomy, which is structurally framed as Moses' farewell speeches (mediated third-person discourse), not verbatim first-person divine speech.</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: large;">• Deuteronomy 2:34 (Retelling of Sihon's Defeat)</span></b>:</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;"> • Text</span></b>: "And we captured all his cities at that time and devoted to destruction (herem) every city, men, women, and children (taf). We left no survivor."</p><p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;"> • Analysis</span></b>: Whereas YHWH merely tells Moses in v. 31 to "Begin to take possession," <b><i><span style="font-size: medium;">Moses retroactively narrates the campaign as a total slaughter of "men, women, and children (taf)"—adding details of women and young children (taf) absent from both MT and SP in Numbers 21:24 (where MT mentions only Sihon, and SP mentions only "his sons"). (Identical in MT and SP regarding this motif)</span></i></b>.</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: large;">• Deuteronomy 3:6 (Retelling of Og's Defeat)</span></b>:</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;"> • Text</span></b>: "And we devoted them to destruction (herem), as we did to Sihon the king of Heshbon, devoting to destruction every city, men, women, and children (taf)."</p><p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;"> • Analysis</span></b>: Moses expands upon YHWH's brief instruction in v. 2 ("do to him as you did to Sihon"), claiming the slaughter of children and directly contradicting the shorter SP witness in Numbers 21:35. (Identical in MT and SP regarding this motif).</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: large;">• Deuteronomy 7:2 (Command Regarding the Seven Canaanite Nations)</span></b>:</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;"> • Text</span></b>: "...and when YHWH your God gives them over to you, and you defeat them, then you must devote them to complete destruction (herem). You shall make no covenant with them and show no mercy to them."</p><p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;"> • Analysis</span></b>: Mediated Mosaic address referring to YHWH in the third person ("YHWH your God"). (Identical in MT and SP regarding this motif).</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: large;">• Deuteronomy 13:15 [MT/SP 13:16] (Law of the Apostate Israelite City)</span></b>:</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;"> • Text</span></b>: "...you shall surely put the inhabitants of that city to the sword, devoting it to destruction (herem), all who are in it..."</p><p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;"> • Analysis</span></b>: Mediated Mosaic legislation applying internal religious herem to an idolatrous Israelite town, implicitly encompassing all inhabitants. (Identical in MT and SP regarding this motif).</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: large;">• Deuteronomy 20:14 and 20:16-17 (Laws of Warfare)</span></b>:</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;"> • Distant Cities (v. 14)</span></b>: "...but the women and the little ones (taf)... you shall take as plunder for yourselves." (Children are spared).</p><p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;"> • Canaanite Cities (vv. 16-17)</span></b>: "But in the cities of these peoples that YHWH your God is giving you for an inheritance, you shall save alive nothing that breathes (kol-neshamah), but you shall devote them to complete destruction (herem)... as YHWH your God has commanded you."</p><p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;"> • Analysis</span></b>: Mediated Mosaic speech attributing the radicalization of herem (killing "nothing that breathes," implicitly including infants and children) to YHWH in the third person. (Identical in MT and SP regarding this motif).</p>
<p><br /><b><i><span style="font-size: medium;"> • (Note: Chapters 21 through 34 of Deuteronomy contain no further warfare commands regarding enemy children)</span></i></b>.</p>
<p><br /></p><h2 style="text-align: left;">Conclusion</h2><p></p>
<p>A strict, literal reading of the Pentateuch clears YHWH of any verbatim, first-person command to kill children in warfare. Across all five books, every relevant passage resolves clearly under textual and narratological scrutiny: in Exodus 23 and 34, YHWH personally drives out the inhabitants while limiting Israel's destruction strictly to pagan idols; in Numbers, the instances involving children stem from a human vow (21:2-3), a Masoretic scribal addition absent in the Samaritan Pentateuch (21:35), or an unprompted directive spoken solely by Moses (31:17); and in Deuteronomy, the radicalized herem commands targeting children appear exclusively within Moses' mediated, third-person speeches. Ultimately, within the Pentateuch itself, warfare against children is a product of Mosaic rhetoric and scribal transmission rather than direct divine speech from Sinai.</p><p><br /></p><p><i><b><span style="font-size: medium;">Note on Samaritan Pentateuch (SP) reference</span></b>:&nbsp;</i></p><p><i>All English renderings and phonetic transliterations from the Samaritan Pentateuch throughout this article—including proper names and tribal designations (such as Shehmaa, Kaanannee, Ehmarree, Ihttee, Girgeshee, Ferizzee, Ibbee, Yeboosee)—follow Benyamim Tsedaka, trans. and ed., <b><span style="font-size: medium;">The Israelite Samaritan Version of the Torah: First English Translation Compared with Masoretic Text Version (Grand Rapids, MI: Eerdmans, 2013)</span></b>, which reflects traditional Samaritan Hebrew pronunciation.</i></p><p></p>
