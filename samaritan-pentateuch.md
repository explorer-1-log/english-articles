Introduction</h2>
<p><br />Conventional discourse in ancient scriptural studies—both within Rabbinic Judaism and Christianity—has long been dominated by the framework of the "Southern Block" (the Kingdom of Judah centered in Jerusalem). This dominance is evident in the widespread reliance on the Masoretic Text (MT), the Septuagint (LXX), and the Dead Sea Scrolls (DSS), all of which are rooted in Southern historical and theological traditions, including interpretations from later prophetic and historical books (Nevi'im and Ketuvim). Achieving an objective understanding of the original jurisprudential philosophy of the ancient Israelites requires a paradigm shift. This study narrows its scope exclusively to the first five books (the Pentateuch) and elevates the Samaritan Pentateuch (SP)—the sole complete textual tradition from the "Northern Block" (the Kingdom of Israel / Samaria)—as a check-and-balance document. Through this approach, the legal structure of the Pentateuch emerges as a closed, rational territorial legal system, free from the religious-political polemics of later eras.</p><p><br /></p>
<h2 style="text-align: left;">2. Methodology and Scope of Study</h2>
<p><br />• The scope of research is restricted 100% to the Pentateuchal text (Genesis through Deuteronomy) without importing interpretations from Southern historical or prophetic books.</p><p><br />• The SP is utilized as a balancing reference text because it preserves the Northern perspective and rejects the auxiliary Southern canon constructed to legitimize the Davidic dynasty and the Jerusalem Temple.</p><p><br />• The discovery of proto-Samaritan manuscripts among the Dead Sea Scrolls confirms that the SP textual tradition existed legitimately in antiquity and was not merely a late alteration.</p><p><br /></p>
<h2 style="text-align: left;">3. Division of Geographical Jurisdiction (Jurisdictional Space)</h2>
<p><br />The legal system in the Pentateuch is not a universal imperial code forcing the entire world to adopt Israelite religion; rather, it is bound to geographical boundaries divided into two primary zones:</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: large;">A. General Space (Cosmic / General Space - Outside Canaanite Borders)</span></b></p><p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;">• Legal Basis</span></b>: Fundamental Humanitarian Law (Natural/Universal Law, Genesis 9:4–6) which prohibits mass violence (Chamas), extreme tyranny, and war crimes.</p><p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;">• Immunity of Foreign Nations</span></b>: Nations outside the specific territory possess the freedom to practice their own social systems and beliefs without Israelite intervention (Deuteronomy 2:4–23 prohibits the Israelites from encroaching upon the territories of Edom, Moab, and Ammon).</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;">• Case Study 1 - The Generation of Noah (Cosmic Scope):</span></b> Punished not for ritual infractions, but for Chamas (physical violence and pervasive bloodshed) that corrupted the earthly ecosystem (Genesis 6:11–13). After the flood, the first basic criminal law code was enacted (Genesis 9).</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;">• Case Study 2 - Misrayim / Egypt (Foreign Empire Scope)</span></b>: Punished for systemic oppression, the targeted killing of male infants (genocide), and Pharaoh's denial of God's sovereignty which demanded the liberation of His servants (Exodus 1–14). The judgment aimed to dismantle a system of tyranny rather than total annihilation.</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;">• Case Study 3 - The Amalekites (Transition Zone / Negev Wilderness)</span></b>: Punished for war crimes because they attacked the weak, the elderly, and vulnerable Israelite civilians from the rear while they were exhausted upon leaving Egypt (Deuteronomy 25:17–19).</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: large;">B. Sacred Space (Eretz Canaan - Specific Territory)</span></b></p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;">• Legal Basis</span></b>: Territorial Purity Laws (Land-Pollution Jurisprudence, Leviticus 18 &amp; Deuteronomy) and Covenantal Contract Law (The Torah).</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;">• Concept of Land as a Moral Entity</span></b>: The land of Canaan belongs absolutely to God and operates with an automatic rejection mechanism. If its occupants pollute the moral ecology of the land, the land will "vomit out" its inhabitants regardless of their ethnicity (Leviticus 18:24–28).</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;">• Case Study 1 - Sodom and Gomorrah (Territorial Precedent)</span></b>: Original inhabitants of Canaan (descendants of Ham/Canaan, Genesis 10:19; referred to as Sadem and Emirra in SP tradition). Though lacking a written Torah, they breached extreme moral boundaries (social tyranny and violence against guests/foreigners) within the borders of the sacred space. Consequently, the land was permanently sterilized with sulfur and fire (Genesis 19:24–25).</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;">• Case Study 2 - The Seven Canaanite Nations (Previous Inhabitants)</span></b>: Judged under laws of territorial eviction and total separation (Cherem, Deuteronomy 20:16–18) because their culture normalized child sacrifice in fire and extreme moral corruption after being granted a long waiting period (Genesis 15:16).</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;">• Case Study 3 - The Israelites (New Contractual Inhabitants):</span></b> Status as a covenant people grants no legal immunity. The Torah contract functions as an "operating manual" to preserve territorial sanctity. If the Israelites breach the contract and imitate the crimes of the original Canaanite inhabitants or Sodom, the covenant curses dictate that their land will be burned like Sodom and they will suffer exile (Deuteronomy 29:22–28).</p><p><br /></p>
<h2 style="text-align: left;">4. Structural Jurisprudential Advantages of the Samaritan Pentateuch (SP) over the Masoretic Text (MT)</h2>
<p><br />When examining the Pentateuch in isolation from Southern sources, the SP displays a more complete and consistent legal logic:</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;">• Certainty of the Sacred Center</span></b>: The SP explicitly names Mount Gerizim as the administrative and sacred center within the Pentateuchal text itself (Deuteronomy 11:29, 27:4), whereas the MT leaves it anonymous ("the place which the Lord will choose") to open the door for the Jerusalem narrative later on.</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;">• Verbal Tense</span></b>: The SP employs the past tense ("the place which the Lord has chosen"), proving that the sanctity of the territory preexisted since the time of the Patriarchs (Abraham and Jacob) and was already in force before the Israelites entered (explaining why Sodom and the Canaanite nations were already subject to that territorial law). The MT uses the future tense ("will choose").</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;">• Constitutional Status</span></b>: The SP integrates the command to build the plastered stone altar of the law on Mount Gerizim directly into the Ten Commandments (Exodus 20 and Deuteronomy 5), establishing it as core constitutional law. The MT relegates it to an ordinary administrative statute at the end of the book (Deuteronomy 27) and relocates the site to Mount Ebal (the mountain of curse).</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;">• Textual Harmonization</span></b>: The SP strictly harmonizes initial commands with final execution to prevent interpretive ambiguity, making the Pentateuch a self-contained, complete, and autonomous legal document.</p><p><br /></p>
<h2 style="text-align: left;">5. Conclusion</h2>
<p><br />Overall, an objective examination of the Pentateuchal text demonstrates that its legal system operates on structured territorial justice rather than arbitrary colonization or reckless judgment. Geography dictates legal jurisdiction: General Space outside Canaan is protected in its sovereignty and is only acted upon when extreme humanitarian atrocities occur (Noah, Misrayim, Amalek), whereas Sacred Space inside Canaan is subject to strict spatial purity laws binding all occupants without exception (Sodom, the Seven Canaanite Nations, and the Israelites). By stripping away Southern block biases and examining the legal structures through the Samaritan Pentateuch, it becomes clear that this text constitutes a consistent constitution, complete with definitive sacred center coordinates, and firmly asserts that chosen-people status never granted the Israelites legal immunity whenever they breached the moral boundaries of that territory.</p>
