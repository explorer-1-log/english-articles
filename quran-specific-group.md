<h2 style="text-align: left;">INTRODUCTION</h2>
<p>For centuries, conventional theology has operated under a mechanical blueprint that views the Quranic narrative of Adam as a universal biological origin story—framing Adam as the initial physical precursor to all homo sapiens across the planet. A primary casualty of this traditional misinterpretation is Surah Al-A'raf 7:172, which is routinely decontextualized to claim a cosmic, biological pre-existence testimony involving the entire global population of human souls. However, a strict, data-driven analysis utilizing a natural Quranic semantic lens (a text-alone approach) exposes severe internal contradictions within this traditional framework. By parsing the exact vocabulary, structural context, and relational boundaries embedded within the scriptural corpus, a profound paradigm shift emerges: the texts do not outline a global biological manual, but rather a specialized legal document. This paper establishes that Adam represents a highly specific, selected subset of an already existing human entity, and that the ensuing legal covenants—including the Torah, Gospel, and Quran—were explicitly designed as governing statutes for this designated lineage within a specific geo-cultural block, carrying no baseline legal liability for populations outside this boundary.</p><p><br /></p><h2 style="text-align: left;">1. THE PARADIGM SHIFT: ADAM AS A SUBSET</h2><p></p>
<p><span style="font-size: medium;">• <b>Traditionalist View</b></span>: Regards Adam as the absolute biological father and the mechanical origin of the entire human species without exception across the globe.</p>
<p><br /><span style="font-size: medium;">• <b>New Perspective (Quranic Semantic)</b></span>: Adam is not the biological representative of all humanity, but rather a specific "subset" or distinct phase selected (istafa) from the larger pre-existing population of Al-Insan / Al-Ins on Earth.</p><p><br /></p>
<h2 style="text-align: left;">2. TEXTUAL EVIDENCE OF THE PROPER NOUN "ADAM" (آدَم)</h2>
<p><span style="font-size: medium;">• <b>Textual Statistics</b></span>: The proper noun Adam (آدَم) occurs 25 times in the Quran, functioning strictly as a proper noun for a highly specific subject in every single instance.</p>
<p><br /><span style="font-size: medium;">• <b>Surah Ali 'Imran (3:33)</b></span>: The text explicitly states, "<b>Indeed, Allah chose (istafa) Adam and Noah</b>...".</p>
<p><br /><span style="font-size: medium;">• <b>Natural Semantic Analysis</b></span>: The verb 'istafa' linguistically means to choose or select a subject out of an already existing pool or collective population. This concept of selection is logically impossible if Adam were the sole physical human in existence at that time.&nbsp;</p><p><br /></p><p><b><span style="font-size: medium;">• Internal Epistemic Proof (2:30-32)</span></b>: This pre-existing population is further confirmed by the angels' response to the appointment of a khalifah (successor) on earth: "<i>Will You place upon it one who causes corruption therein and sheds blood</i>?" (2:30). Because the text explicitly establishes that the angels possess no independent knowledge of the unseen beyond what they are taught (2:32), their question could not be speculative prophecy. Instead, it reflects empirical observation of an already active population of Al-Insan on earth exhibiting corruption and bloodshed—from which Adam was selected (istafa) as the first accountable subset to succeed them into a phase of moral awareness and legal accountability.&nbsp;</p><p><br /></p>
<h2 style="text-align: left;">3. THE COSMIC AND RELATIONAL SCOPE OF AL-'ALAMIN</h2>
<p><span style="font-size: medium;">• <b>The Nature of Al-'Alamin</b></span>: The Quranic text never conceptualizes the universe through an abstract, independent philosophical lens (such as the Greek Cosmos or the later scholastic Al-Kawn). The existence of creation is presented functionally (what God does) and relationally (how He relates to humanity).</p>
<p><br /><span style="font-size: medium;">• <b>The Binary Boundary</b></span>: The scope of Al-'Alamin dynamically expands or contracts depending entirely on the textual context, alternating between a macroscopic scope (the heavens, the earth, and whatever is between them) and a domestic scope (human societies and historical civilizations).</p>
<p><br /><span style="font-size: medium;">• <b>Implication of the Selection (3:33)</b></span>: When Adam is chosen over 'Al-'Alamin' within the domestic scope of human societies, it confirms the existence of contemporary human social structures that were not selected to bear the same divine legal burdens. The phase of Adam marks the definitive initiation of an active covenant relationship between the Creator and His accountable subset of creation.</p><p><br /></p>
<h2 style="text-align: left;">4. THE GEO-CULTURAL PERIMETER OF "BANI ADAM" AND THE INSTITUTION OF THE MASJID</h2>
<p><span style="font-size: medium;">• <b>Surah Al-A'raf (7:31)</b></span>: The text commands, "<b>O Children of Adam! Take your adornment at every masjid</b>...".</p>
<p><br /><span style="font-size: medium;">• <b>Anthropological Textual Analysis</b></span>: The concept and institution of the 'masjid' (a designated place of prostration/worship rooted in Semitic monotheistic traditions) historically and geographically developed exclusively within the cultural spheres of the Middle East. Ancient global civilizations outside this region (such as the Maya, Inca, or far East Asian societies) did not share this specific institution in their early history.</p>
<p><br /><span style="font-size: medium;">• <b>Conclusion on the Legal Perimeter</b></span>: A command bound to a highly localized cultural symbol (the masjid) proves that the call to 'Bani Adam' refers to a subset of humanity within Adam's <b>kinship and territorial sphere</b>&nbsp;(3:33), not a universal biological label for the global human population.</p><p><br /></p>
<h2 style="text-align: left;">5. THE CONTEXTUAL REALITY OF SURAH AL-A'RAF 7:172</h2>
<p><span style="font-size: medium;">• <b>Traditional Misconception</b></span>: Traditionalists interpret the phrase "<b>when your Lord took from the Children of Adam... their descendants</b>" (7:172) as a global, cosmic extraction of all human souls from the backs of the descendants of Adam at the dawn of time to testify to monotheism.</p>
<p><br /><span style="font-size: medium;">• <b>The Textual Narrative Block</b></span>: From a strict structural perspective, verse 7:172 does not stand as an isolated cosmic dogma. It is deeply embedded right in the middle of an intensive narrative block tracking the history, covenants, and repeated legal failures of Bani Israel (spanning continuously from verse 7:59 onwards).</p>
<p><br /><span style="font-size: medium;">• <b>Impossibility of Universal Application</b></span>: Forcing a universal biological testimony involving global humanity (e.g., ancient Amazonian or Arctic populations) into the middle of a specific, localized warning directed at the Israelite community creates a severe contextual rupture. The testimony in 7:172 was exacted from the genealogical line of Bani Adam to hold them legally accountable so they could not claim historical ignorance on the Day of Judgment. Because global human populations outside this perimeter never received the prophetic chains and scriptures to remind them of such a testimony, applying it to them invalidates the legal argument of the text itself. Thus, 7:172 confirms a subset, operating within a subset, for a specific legal group.</p><p><br /></p>
<h2 style="text-align: left;">6. THE CHAIN OF MESSENGERS AND NESTED SUBSETS</h2>
<p><span style="font-size: medium;">• <b>Surah Al-A'raf (7:35)</b></span>: The text declares, <b>"O Children of Adam! If there come to you messengers from among you (rusulun minkum)</b>...". The historical chain of messengers mentioned throughout the scriptural corpus operated strictly within the Middle Eastern region. Therefore, the target audience of 'Bani Adam' naturally shrinks to the specific subset of the population living within the radius of these messengers.</p>
<p><br /><span style="font-size: medium;">• <b>Surah Maryam (19:58)</b></span>: The text establishes a geometric pattern of nested subsets: from the Descendants of Adam, narrowing to the Descendants of Abraham, and further narrowing to the Descendants of Israel. Since the lineages of Abraham and Israel act as highly specific groups rather than universal populations, structural consistency dictates that the Descendants of Adam also represent a specific subset of the global human population.</p><p><br /></p>
<h2 style="text-align: left;">7. THE LEGAL MECHANISM: THE COVENANT ('AHD / MITHAQ)</h2>
<p><span style="font-size: medium;">• <b>Surah Yasin (36:60)</b></span>: The text utilizes the phrase, "<b>Did I not enjoin upon you / make a covenant with you, O Children of Adam (a'had ilaikum ya Bani Adama), that you not worship Satan?</b>...".</p>
<p><br /><span style="font-size: medium;">• <b>Surah Al-Baqarah (2:27)</b></span>: Couples the concept of the covenant ('Ahd) with a legally binding contract (Mithaq).</p>
<p><br /><span style="font-size: medium;">• <b>The Semantic Logic of a Covenant</b></span>: A binding legal covenant ('Ahd/Mithaq) cannot semantically or logically operate upon subjects who are completely unaware of it, lack linguistic access to it, or exist entirely outside the system. The title 'Bani Adam' is bound to this formal genealogical covenant, meaning they constitute a specific legal group within its legal perimeter, automatically excluding external human populations who were never party to the agreement.</p><p><br /></p>
<h2 style="text-align: left;">8. ESTABLISHING THE GOAL: THE QURAN IS A DOCUMENT "FOR THEM"</h2>
<p><span style="font-size: medium;">• <b>Surah Al-Hajj (22:78)</b></span>: The text locks this status through absolute phrasing, "...<b>He has chosen you (huwa-jtabakum)... the religion of your father Abraham. He named you 'Al-Muslimin' from before</b>...".</p>
<p><br /><span style="font-size: medium;">• <b>The Concept of Absolute Selection (The Chosen Ones)</b></span>: The root verb 'ijtaba' means to separate, isolate, and filter an entity out from a larger collective. This represents a sovereign, authoritative decree (Divine Selection), not a voluntary or open public enrollment.</p>
<p><br /><span style="font-size: medium;">• <b>Final Conclusion on Target Audience</b></span>: Based on the structural analysis of commands, obligations, and prohibitions, the Quran is explicitly constructed <b><span style="font-size: medium;">FOR THEM—the specific, designated group selected from the population, bound to the legal lineage of Adam and Abraham</span></b>. The text carries no legal liability (taklif) for external nations or global populations falling outside this lineage, though they remain free to derive passive moral lessons and universal insights from it.</p><p><br /></p>
<h2 style="text-align: left;">9. THE CONSISTENCY OF THE MODEL WITH THE TRADITIONS OF THE TORAH AND GOSPEL</h2>
<p><span style="font-size: medium;">• <b>A Unified Pattern</b></span>: When analyzing the Torah and the Gospel from the perspective of <span style="font-size: medium;">"<b>their" own historical, Semitic traditions</b></span>, the structural blueprint matches the Quran with 100% consistency.</p>
<p><br /><span style="font-size: medium;">• <b>The Torah as a Family-Nation Law</b></span>: An exclusive <b><span style="font-size: medium;">covenant between YHWH and the descendants of Jacob (Bani Israel)</span></b>, carrying no legal liability for outside nations.</p><p><br /><span style="font-size: medium;">• <b>The Gospel as an Internal Restoration</b></span>: Early textual layers explicitly restrict its semantic focus to "<b><span style="font-size: medium;">the lost sheep of the house of Israel</span></b>."</p>
<p><br /><span style="font-size: medium;">• <b>Macro Summary</b></span>: All three scriptures (Torah, Gospel, Quran) share the exact same structural model, the same geographic and prophetic chain, and the same binding mechanism ('Ahd/Mithaq) to govern a <b><span style="font-size: medium;">specific, chosen group</span></b>, completely refuting the notion of a universal, biological human manual.</p><p><br /></p>

Grounding Surah Al-A'raf 7:172 back into its structural narrative home among the Israelites exposes the fallacy of global soul-extraction theories. The identification of Adam as a functional subset within the broader pool of humanity unlocks a consistent, contradiction-free reading of scriptural geography, anthropology, and law. <b><span style="font-size: medium;">Ultimately, this framework firmly establishes that the Quran—in its capacity as a system of absolute commands, limits, and legal obligations—was definitively authored for them: the designated, covenanted group</span></b>. While the universal essence of human traits remains distinct under the category of Al-Insan, legal accountability under this specific divine law is confined <b><span style="font-size: medium;">strictly to its intended lineage</span></b>, leaving the rest of global humanity completely free from its direct statutory liability.</p>
