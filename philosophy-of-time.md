<h2>🌌 1. The Titan Concept &amp; Mortal Kombat (MK) Lore Errors</h2>
<div><strong>1. Mythological Origins</strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<div></div>
<p>The name "Titan" originates from Greek mythology, referring to the early generation of giant deities (symbolizing the raw forces of nature) before they were overthrown by the Olympian Gods (who symbolized law and civilization).</p><p><br /></p>
<div><strong>2. The Myth of Immortality</strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<div></div>
<p>In original Greek texts, Titans were Athanatoi (immortal beings incapable of dying). They were not killed by Zeus, but were instead imprisoned in Tartarus. Modern pop culture frequently misdepicts them as being physically mortal.</p><p><br /></p>
<div><strong>3. The Lore Paradox of MK11 &amp; MK1</strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<div></div>
<p>Game developers promoted Titans above the Elder Gods, positioning them as keepers of the timeline (multiverse) using the Hourglass device. From a storytelling perspective, this concept kills the impact of character suffering and breaks the law of cause and effect (causality) since timelines can be replayed at will.</p>
<h2>🪐 2. Cosmic Unity: The Cosmic Giant Archetype</h2>
<p>The concept in Mortal Kombat where the universe is formed from the severed body pieces of The One Being—cut down by the Elder Gods—draws direct inspiration from ancient mythological archetypes of world creation through the corpse of a primordial being:</p><p><br /></p>
<div><strong>1. Indian Mythology (Rigveda</strong>)</div><div><br /></div>
<p>This concept mirrors the story of Purusha, where the entire cosmos, stars, gods, and social systems were born from the sacrificial body of this original Cosmic Giant.</p><p><br /></p>
<div><strong>2. Ancient Chinese Mythology</strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<p>The tale of Pangu, where after his death, his breath became the wind, his eyes turned into the sun and moon, and his body formed the physical landscape.</p><p><br /></p>
<div><strong>3. Norse Mythology (Scandinavian)</strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<p>The story of the giant Ymir, who was slain by Odin and his brothers; his blood became the oceans, his flesh became the earth, and his skull formed the sky.</p><p><br /></p>
<div><strong>4. Function of the MK Tournament</strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<p>The mandatory requirement to win 10 consecutive tournaments to conquer a realm serves as a "cosmic brake." It prevents the physical matter of the planets from merging, which would otherwise reawaken The One Being (triggering the Apocalypse).</p>
<h2>⏳ 3. Philosophy of Time: An Uncontrollable Ultimate Reality</h2>
<p>Time is often depicted as the ultimate entity that binds everything together, rather than a toy to be manipulated:</p><p><br /></p>
<div><strong>1. Persian Mythology (Zurvanism)</strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<p>The school of Zurvanism positions Time (Zurvan) as the supreme entity and cosmic womb that gave birth to the forces of good (Ahura Mazda) and evil (Ahriman).</p><p><br /></p>
<div><strong>2. Pre-Islamic Arabian Tradition (Ad-Dahr)</strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<p>Ancient Arabs personified time (Ad-Dahr) as an absolute force governing fate and bringing death to humans—a concept that was later corrected in Islamic theology.</p>
<h2>🕰️ 4. The Evolution of Clock Technology: Attempts to Tame Time</h2>
<p>The human desire to control time is reflected in the evolution of timekeeping devices, shifting from human error to automated precision:</p><p><br /></p>
<div><strong>1. The Hourglass (Sand Clock)</strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<p>Evolving from the ancient water clock (Clepsydra) of the ancient Middle East, the dual-glass bulb design was perfected during the High Middle Ages. It was inefficient for long durations due to human lag during flipping (turnover lag) and keeper negligence.</p><p><br /></p>
<div><strong>2. The Pendulum Clock (1656)</strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<p>Invented by Christiaan Huygens based on Galileo's principle of isochronism. This clock utilized the law of gravity (iron weights) and a specific pendulum length (approximately 0.994 meters to produce an exact 1-second swing) to deliver highly accurate and consistent automated ticking.</p>
<h2>🧠 5. The Battle of Time &amp; Space Doctrines: Ancient Philosophy vs. Modern Physics Chronology</h2>
<p>Debates regarding the nature of time and space within Eastern philosophical schools demonstrate a chronology of discourse that occurred long before being supported by modern theoretical physics:</p><p><br /></p>
<div><strong>1. Theravada School (Doctrines of Presentism &amp; Momentariness)</strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<p>Believes that only the Present truly exists. The past is already destroyed and the future is not yet born. Matter and consciousness flow at extreme speeds within micro-moments termed Momentariness. This ancient school parallels the modern physics theory of Presentism (only the infinitesimal present moment is real).</p><p><br /></p>
<div><strong>2. Sarvastivada School (Tri-temporal Doctrine)</strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<p>Believes that all three dimensions of time (Past, Present, Future) exist simultaneously (Tri-temporal). They argue that matter possesses an intrinsic nature that exists continuously across time; the present is merely its active phase. This ancient school parallels the modern physics theory of the Block Universe / Fabric of Spacetime (where past, present, and future coexist within the spacetime fabric).</p><p><br /></p>
<div><strong>3. Madhyamaka School (Doctrine of Sunyata / Emptiness)</strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<p>Pioneered by figures like Nagarjuna, who believed that time is an illusion and lacks its own independent physical entity. Time is merely a conceptual construct of the mind that depends entirely on the changes of other things (if nothing changes, time does not exist). This ancient school parallels modern theoretical physics which suggests that time does not exist at all at the fundamental atomic level.</p>
<h2>🌌 6. The Akasa (Space) Controversy Between Theravada vs. Sarvastivada</h2>
<div></div>
<div><strong>1. The Debate Over Space</strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<div></div>
<p>The aforementioned debate on time eventually dragged into the issue of the nature of empty space (Akasa).</p><p><br /></p>
<div><strong>2. The Sarvastivada Stance</strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<div></div>
<p>Sarvastivada claimed that Akasa is Asankhata (unconditioned/eternal) across the three dimensions of time to allow matter to move.</p><p><br /></p>
<div><strong>3. The Theravada Refutation</strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<div></div>
<p>This view was strongly opposed by the Theravada school (Textual references: Kv 6.6 and Kv 6.4). Theravada argued that only ultimate liberation (Nibbana) is Asankhata. Space (Akasa), on the other hand, is merely a conceptual construct of the mind used to measure the distance between matter, rather than an eternal physical entity (Cross-referenced textual commentary: Dhs. 638).</p>
<h2>📐 7. Two Perspectives &amp; The Role of Consciousness in Understanding Space</h2>
<p>To delve deeper into the Akasa controversy, the reality of space must technically be understood through the relationship between consciousness and objects:</p><p><br /></p>
<div><strong>1. The Distance Perspective (Macro/External)</strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<p>A practical viewpoint where space is seen as a void or empty zone that establishes distance between one object and another (from Object A to Object B).</p><p><br /></p>
<div><strong>2. The Boundary Perspective (Micro/Internal)</strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<p>A philosophical text viewpoint where space is viewed as the limit or boundary of every piece of matter. Space acts as a dividing line that determines where a certain matter begins and ends, thereby preventing all atoms in the universe from merging into a single, shapeless, dense mass.</p><p><br /></p>
<div><strong>3. Dependence on Consciousness and Objects (Epistemology)</strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<p>From a technical standpoint, the existence of space requires the consciousness of objects. The human mind cannot detect "emptiness" directly without a reference point. A dimension is only designated as "space" once human consciousness first detects the presence of matter and subsequently processes the gaps or boundaries between those objects. Without the consciousness of physical objects (or their absence) serving as a reference point, the cognitive function of the mind would not produce what we call "space."</p>
<h2>👁️ 8. Philosophy of Perception &amp; Cognitive Integration</h2>
<p>How humans process reality in space and time is the result of a complex combination of sensory systems:</p><p><br /></p>
<div><strong>1. Multisensory Integration (Sight + Hearing)</strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<p>The brain's blending of visual and auditory cues is not merely additive (1+1=2). Instead, it creates a new cognitive reality that is far more efficient (1+1=3) for understanding the environment.</p><p><br /></p>
<div><strong>2. Dimensional Property Differences Between Visual and Auditory Inputs</strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<p><strong>I. Sight Only</strong>: Dominant in mapping Space, distance, and shape, but loses the emotional dimension or atmosphere without sound.</p><p><br /></p>
<p><strong>II. Hearing Only</strong>: Dominant in mapping Time and emotion, but forces the mind to guess the physical orientation and direction of an object.</p><p><br /></p>
<div><strong>3. The McGurk Effect</strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<p>Cognitive science evidence demonstrating how visual input (watching a different mouth movement) can trick and alter what is physically heard by the ears. The brain constantly attempts to unify different sensory inputs into a single mental vessel (the Mind) to construct a cohesive phenomenal experience.</p>
<h2>🏁 9. Grand Conclusion</h2>
<p>Time and space constitute a fundamental cosmic vessel or dimension. They are not items, gadgets, or powers that can be controlled according to a creature's whims (contrary to the errors depicted in Mortal Kombat fiction). Whether the flow of time is linear or cyclical, the universe's causal flow (cause and effect) operates strictly one-way on a macro scale, binding all entities without exception.</p>
