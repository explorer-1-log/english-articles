<h2 style="text-align: left;">Introduction</h2>
<p><br />To understand the true purpose of why the Torah law was given to the descendants of Israel, the investigation must be strictly confined to the text of the Pentateuch (Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers, and Deuteronomy) based on the Masoretic Text (MT) and the Samaritan Pentateuch (SP) traditions, without filtering them through external interpretations. When the direct speech of YHWH in the Pentateuch is traced chronologically from the Book of Exodus through the Book of Numbers, a clear, closed, and exclusive physical legal framework emerges. This framework stands in absolute contradiction to the New Testament (New Covenant) narrative, as well as the concepts of redemption within Rabbinic Judaism and Christianity that re-read the Torah through an external eschatological lens.</p>
<p><br /></p><h2 style="text-align: left;">The True Purpose of Giving the Torah Law According to the Direct Speech of YHWH</h2><p>Based on the chronology of the Pentateuch (MT and SP), YHWH gave the Torah law to the descendants of Israel not as a trap to bring death or merely to awaken human consciousness of sin, but for tangible covenantal reasons and physical survival:</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;">1. Test of Obedience and Physical Protection (Book of Exodus)</span></b>: Before arriving at Mount Sinai, YHWH began issuing decrees at Marah and the Wilderness of Sin as a test of obedience (Exodus 16:4) and as a condition for protection against the diseases that afflicted Egypt (Exodus 15:26).</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;">2. Covenant Terms and Exclusive Identity (Book of Exodus)</span></b>: At Mount Sinai, YHWH stated the overarching purpose of giving His word and covenant—to make the descendants of Israel His treasured possession (segullah) out of all peoples, a kingdom of priests, and a holy nation (Exodus 19:5–6). Furthermore, laws and ordinances were given to be taught to them (Exodus 24:12), to prevent them from being ensnared by the deities of the Canaanites (Exodus 23:32–33), and to serve as holiness protocols so that YHWH could dwell physically in their midst (Exodus 25:8; 29:45–46).</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;">3. Sanctification, Separation, and Life in the Promised Land (Book of Leviticus)</span></b>: In the Book of Leviticus, YHWH explicitly affirms that the law was given so that the descendants of Israel would sanctify themselves to match the holiness of YHWH (Leviticus 11:44–45; 19:2) and remain strictly separated from the cultural practices of Egypt and Canaan in order to belong to Him (Leviticus 18:3; 20:24–26). YHWH also declares that His laws were given so that a person may live by them (Leviticus 18:5), attain material prosperity in the land of Canaan (Leviticus 26:3–12), and prevent the land from vomiting them out due to defilement (Leviticus 18:28).</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;">4. Prevention of Sin and Preservation of Life (Book of Numbers)</span></b>: Throughout the wilderness journey, YHWH states that His statutes (such as the law of tassels or tzitzit) serve as visual reminders so that the descendants of Israel do not follow the lusts of their own hearts and eyes that lead to spiritual infidelity (Numbers 15:39–40). Regulations regarding camp boundaries and Levitical duties were established to maintain the purity of YHWH's dwelling space (Numbers 5:2–3) and to act as a protective buffer zone so that the Israelites would not die from encroaching upon His holiness (Numbers 18:5, 22).</p>
<p><br /></p><h2 style="text-align: left;">Contradictions of New Testament Interpretations Regarding Adam and the Nature of the Torah</h2><p></p>
<p><br />Interpretations within the New Testament—whether oriented toward past or future eschatological fulfillment—frequently claim that the law was given because of Adam's fall, was designed to be broken to provoke sin-consciousness, functioned temporarily like a vehicle from one point to another, and aimed to abolish boundary-driven thinking among peoples. When filtered strictly through the Pentateuch, this entire narrative contradicts the original text on four primary grounds:</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;">1. No Connection Between the Narrative of Adam and the Sinai Torah</span></b>: In Genesis 2:16–17, YHWH gave only a single verbal prohibition to Adam regarding the tree of the knowledge of good and evil. The word Torah does not exist in Eden, and in Genesis 3:17, YHWH cursed the ground, rather than transmitting inherited "original sin" into the souls of all humanity. When YHWH delivered the Torah to Israel from Exodus through Numbers, Adam is never mentioned; the historical anchor of the Torah is always rooted in the covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and the deliverance from Egypt. Moreover, sin-consciousness already existed prior to Sinai, as evidenced by the confessions of Cain (Genesis 4:13) and Pharaoh (Exodus 9:27).</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;">2. Law Given to Be Obeyed and Give Life, Not to Be Broken</span></b>: The claim that the law was deliberately designed to be transgressed and bring death directly contradicts YHWH's declaration in Leviticus 18:5 that the one who does them shall live by them, and Numbers 15:39 that the commandments were given so that they would not sin.</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;">3. Perpetual Statutes, Not a Temporary Vehicle</span></b>: The Pentateuch never establishes an expiration date for the Torah nor promises a phase of spiritual consciousness without law. On the contrary, YHWH proclaims foundational ordinances such as Passover, the Sabbath, the Day of Atonement, and the annual festivals as eternal statutes forever (chuqqat olam) for the descendants of Israel (Exodus 12:14; 31:16–17; Leviticus 16:34; 23:41).</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;">4. Boundary Markers Established by YHWH Himself</span></b>: Boundary-driven thinking that distinguishes Israel from other nations (Goyim) is not a human ego construct, but a direct command from YHWH in Leviticus 20:26, which separates the descendants of Israel from the peoples so that they might belong to Him.</p>
<p><br /></p><h2 style="text-align: left;">The Crisis of the Severed Physical System of the Pentateuch and the Chain of Ritual Prerequisites</h2><p></p>
<p><br />Although the Torah remains the binding covenantal standard for the descendants of Israel, the execution of its purification laws has been technically halted because the Pentateuch is a closed physical legal system entirely dependent on four interlocking prerequisites:</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;">1. Absolute Dependence on the Ohel Mo'ed and the Ark of the Covenant</span></b>: According to Leviticus 16, the annual purification from all sins and ritual impurities (Yom Kippur) for the entire congregation of Israel is valid only when executed inside the Tent of Meeting (Ohel Mo'ed) built according to the Mount Sinai pattern (Exodus 25:40), before the Ark of the Covenant equipped with the mercy seat (kaporet), involving the sprinkling of sacrificial blood and the dispatching of the Azazel goat into the wilderness. Without the Ohel Mo'ed and the Ark of the Covenant, the ritual of Leviticus 16:30 and the purification from corpse impurity using the ashes of the red heifer (Numbers 19) are impossible to perform, leaving the annual ledger of sins for the descendants of Israel unpurged.</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;">2. Absence of the Fire from Before YHWH</span></b>: On the day of the Tabernacle's inauguration, fire came out from before YHWH to consume the offering (Leviticus 9:24). This divine fire was required to be kept burning perpetually while encamped as well as while transported by the Kohathites during travel (Leviticus 6:12–13; Numbers 4:13–15), serving as the sole legitimate source of coals for burning incense before the Ark of the Covenant on Yom Kippur (Leviticus 16:12–13). Using human-kindled fire is classified as "strange fire" (esh zarah) punishable by death (Leviticus 10:1–2). The loss of the original Tabernacle means the loss of this original divine fire.</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;">3. Absence of a Legally Qualified Anointed Priest (Mashiach)</span></b>: Throughout the entire Pentateuch (MT and SP), the title Mashiach never refers to a king or an eschatological savior; instead, it appears only four times in the Book of Leviticus (Leviticus 4:3, 5, 16; 6:22) referring exclusively to the Aaronic High Priest (ha-kohen ha-mashiach). Qualification as a Mashiach under the Torah does not rely merely on Aaron's biological lineage, but requires a seven-day consecration at the entrance of the Ohel Mo'ed using sacrificial blood and the sacred anointing oil, whose formula is strictly forbidden from being reproduced outside the original command (Exodus 29:4–7; 30:31–33; Leviticus 8). Without the Ohel Mo'ed and the authentic anointing oil, no individual on earth today can legally hold the office of High Priest.</p>
<p><br /><b><span style="font-size: medium;">4. Exclusion of the Stone Temple Concept and the Davidic Dynasty from the Sinai Blueprint</span></b>: The Pentateuch never commands the construction of a permanent stone Temple in Jerusalem nor ties the atonement system to the rule of a king from the tribe of Judah. The Sinai blueprint was exclusively for a portable Tabernacle, and the phrase "the place that YHWH chooses" in Deuteronomy 12 refers to the permanent site for that Tabernacle in the land of Canaan (left unnamed in the Southern MT tradition, or explicitly anchored to Mount Gerizim in the Northern SP tradition). Therefore, the destruction of the First and Second Temples in Jerusalem represents the collapse of replacement institutions built outside the original specifications of the Pentateuch, as the Second Temple itself never possessed the Ark of the Covenant, the mercy seat, the sacred anointing oil, or the original fire from before YHWH.</p>
<p><br /></p><h2 style="text-align: left;">The Failure of First-Century Alternatives and Doctrinal Errors Regarding the Messiah</h2><p></p>
<p><br />Confronted with the legal deadlock caused by the absence of the Ohel Mo'ed, the Ark of the Covenant, the anointing oil, and the fire of YHWH, various first-century CE movements attempted to engineer alternative solutions. The Essene community at Qumran turned to communal asceticism and separation; the movement of John the Baptist relied on river water immersion; while the teachings of Jesus, the theology of Paul, and the Epistle to the Hebrews in the New Testament attempted to replace the Torah mechanism with doctrines of human blood sacrifice, a heavenly tabernacle, or a priesthood outside the tribe of Levi.</p>
<p><br />When adjudicated strictly by the legal standards of the Pentateuch, all of these initiatives are rendered invalid and stand in contradiction to the original text. The Torah prohibits adding to or subtracting from its commandments (Deuteronomy 4:2), strictly forbids human sacrifice (Deuteronomy 12:31; 24:16), restricts priestly duties exclusively to the descendants of Aaron from the tribe of Levi (Numbers 18:7), and stipulates that life atonement occurs solely through animal blood upon a valid earthly altar (Leviticus 17:11). Furthermore, unlike repentance doctrines in other religions that are internal and whose acceptance remains uncertain, Yom Kippur in Leviticus 16:30 functions as an objective, collective legal mechanism; once that physical ritual ceased, no amount of prayer, charity, or internal remorse can legally substitute for the atoning power of sacrificial blood within the Pentateuch.</p>
<p><br />Simultaneously, both the Rabbinic Jewish concept of the Messiah (as a Davidic royal savior) and the Christian concept (as a sin-bearer and High Priest from the tribe of Judah) deviate from the original definition in the Pentateuch. Within the Torah framework, the descendants of Israel do not need a new savior figure to abolish the law; rather, they have lost access to the only Mashiach recognized by the Pentateuch—the Aaronic High Priest legally anointed at the Ohel Mo'ed.</p>
<p><br /></p><h2 style="text-align: left;">Conclusion</h2><p></p>
<p><br />In summary, based strictly on the text of the Pentateuch alone (MT and SP), the Torah law was given exclusively to the descendants of Israel as covenantal terms to establish their identity as a holy nation, preserve their physical life in the promised land, and enable YHWH to dwell in their midst. The absence of the Ohel Mo'ed, the Ark of the Covenant, the sacred anointing oil, and the fire from before YHWH today has severed the qualification chain of the anointed High Priest (ha-kohen ha-mashiach) and completely immobilized the execution of the Yom Kippur ritual. Every attempt by New Testament movements, Rabbinic Judaism, or first-century reform sects to reconfigure the purpose of the Torah into spiritual allegory, replace it with water rituals, or introduce a Messiah outside the Aaronic priesthood constitutes an external interpretation that fails when tested against the literal standards of the Pentateuch.</p>
