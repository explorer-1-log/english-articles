<h2><strong>1. Defining Words Through a Usage-Based Approach</strong></h2>
<div></div>
<div></div>
<div></div>
<div><strong>Al-Insan /&nbsp;الإنسان&nbsp;= The Species / Humanity</strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<div></div>
<div></div>
<p>- This term refers to mankind as a whole or the universal essence of humanity.</p>
<p>- It addresses the foundational blueprint, existential nature, mental capacities, and biological/psychological traits inherent to the human species, independent of geographical boundaries or eras.</p><p><br /></p>
<div><strong>Al-Nas /&nbsp;الناس&nbsp;= The Society / Collective / Population</strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<div></div>
<div></div>
<p>- This term refers to localized groups of people bound together within a tangible societal system.</p>
<p>- It addresses sociopolitical dynamics, economic interactions, community laws, and the structural order of a specific population within a particular timeline.</p>
<h2><strong>2. Implications for the Prophetic &amp; Apostolic Mission</strong></h2>
<div></div>
<div></div>
<div><strong>Localized Operational Mission</strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<div></div>
<p>- Prophet Muhammad’s practical duty as a Messenger operated strictly within the realm of الناس (Al-Nas). His role was to reform the sociopolitical fabric of the local 7th-century Hijazi Arab society using the language of his own people (as seen in QS. 43:44 and QS. 25:30).</p><p><br /></p>
<div><strong>Universal Message</strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<div></div>
<p>- The core values, knowledge, and warnings within the Quran are directed toward العالمين (Al-`Alamin)—the various nations and civilizations of conscious beings within the broader الإنسان (Al-Insan) species worldwide.</p><p><br /></p>
<div><strong>The Meaning of Kaffatan lin-Nas (QS. 34:28)</strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<div></div>
<p>- Understood synchronically, this phrase means being sent to "all elements and communities of the Arab society at that time" comprehensively, rather than being restricted to a single clan or tribe.</p><p><br /></p>
<div><strong>Absence of Mass Proselytism</strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<div></div>
<p>- Unlike the Gospel concept of the Great Commission (to baptize non-Israelite nations), a Messenger’s operational duty in the Quran is solely to convey information clearly (balagh). It was not a project for the forced religious expansion or conversion of non-Arab ('Ajam) nations. This practical mission concluded entirely upon his passing as the Seal of the Prophets (خاتم النبيين - QS. 33:40).</p>
<h2><strong>3. The Theological Concept of "Muslim" Status (QS. 22:78)</strong></h2>
<div></div>
<div></div>
<div><strong>Identity by Divine Designation</strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<div></div>
<p>- The status and title of "Muslim" is a specific designation chosen and bestowed directly by God upon the immediate followers who fought alongside Prophet Muhammad during his lifetime.</p><p><br /></p>
<div><strong>Not a Voluntary Human Claim</strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<div></div>
<p>- This refutes the modern romanticism of religious conversion or "reverting." In the context of the text, becoming a Muslim is an absolute prerogative of Divine decree, rather than a matter of free human choice in later generations.</p><p><br /></p>
<div><strong>The Meaning of Min Qablu (مِنْ قَبْلُ)</strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<div></div>
<p>- Translating to "before this" or "from the past," this phrase asserts that the naming and isolation of this chosen group's identity was already etched into historical pre-determination by God, long preceding the physical existence of the community itself.</p>
<h2><strong>4. Implications for Modern-Day Sharia Law</strong></h2>
<div></div>
<div></div>
<div><strong>Laws Limited to Contemporary Followers</strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<div></div>
<p>- Technical and sociocultural rules,&nbsp;commands, and prohibitions (hukum taklifi) strictly bound the group of الناس (Al-Nas) who pledged allegiance (bay'ah) to the Prophet during his lifetime.</p><p><br /></p>
<div><strong>Cross-Cultural Flexibility</strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<div></div>
<p>- Modern non-Arab ('Ajam) populations bear no theological obligation to adopt the localized legal culture of 7th-century Arabia. What the world inherits instead is the universal substance of Quranic values—such as justice, morality, and humanity—to be applied organically within their respective local civilizations.</p>
