<div style="text-align: left;"><div><br /></div><div><br /></div><h2 style="text-align: left;">Introduction&nbsp;</h2><div><br /></div><div><i><b>Editor's note (Updated November 11,2026)</b>: Originally published on September 28, 2026. This article has been revised to sharpen its theological and historical framing—specifically emphasizing the concept of <b>divine genealogical selection</b>, the literal ancestral legacy of Abraham's descendants, and the distinction between localized prescriptive laws and universal ethical wisdom.</i></div><div><br /></div><div><br /></div><div>Understanding the precise lexical boundaries of Quranic terminology is essential for grasping the historical and theological scope of its message. By distinguishing between Al-Insan (universal humanity) and Al-Nas (specific societal collectives), we uncover a crucial reality: the operational laws and <b>divine mandates</b> of the Quran were anchored in&nbsp;<b>divine genealogical selection</b>—specifically addressing the descendants of Abraham—while offering universal ethical wisdom to the rest of the world.</div></div><h2><strong>1. Defining Words Through a Usage-Based Approach</strong></h2><div><strong><span style="font-size: large;"><br /></span></strong></div><div><strong><span style="font-size: large;">Al-Insan /&nbsp;الإنسان&nbsp;= Humanity ( The Total Human Entity )</span></strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<div></div>
<div></div>
<p>- <b>This term refers to mankind as a whole or the universal essence of humanity</b>.</p>
<p>- <b>It addresses the foundational blueprint, existential nature, mental capacities, and inherent psychological traits of the human entity</b>, independent of geographical boundaries or eras.</p><p><br /></p>
<div><strong><span style="font-size: large;">Al-Nas /&nbsp;الناس&nbsp;= The Society / Collective / Population</span></strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<div></div>
<div></div>
<p>- <b>This term refers to localized groups of people bound together within a tangible societal system</b>.</p>
<p>- <b>It addresses sociopolitical dynamics, economic interactions, community laws, and the structural order</b> of a specific population within a particular timeline.</p><p><br /></p>
<h2><strong>2. Implications for the Prophetic &amp; Apostolic Mission</strong></h2><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<div></div>
<div></div>
<div><strong><span style="font-size: large;">Localized Operational Mission</span></strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<div></div>
<p>- <b>Prophet Muhammad’s practical duty as a Messenger operated strictly within the realm of Al-Nas (الناس), referring specifically to his immediate Abrahamic kinsfolk</b>. Rather than a global campaign, his role was to reform the sociopolitical fabric of the local 7th-century Hijazi Arab society—exclusively addressing his own people (qawm) in their native tongue (QS. 43:44; QS. 25:30).</p><p><br /></p>
<div><strong><span style="font-size: large;">Universal Message</span></strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<div></div>
<p>- <b>The overarching moral wisdom, knowledge, and existential warnings within the Quran are directed toward Al-'Alamin (العالمين)</b>. this refers to the broader civilizations within the universal Al-Insan (الإنسان) species worldwide, who benefit from its ethical insights without being bound by localized ancestral <b>obligations</b>.&nbsp;</p><p><br /></p>
<div><strong><span style="font-size: large;">The Meaning of Kaffatan lin-Nas (QS. 34:28)</span></strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<div></div>
<p>- <b>Understood synchronically, this phrase means being sent to "all elements and communities of the Arab society at that time" comprehensively.</b> Rather than being restricted to a single clan or tribe, the message encompassed the entire Abrahamic lineage within that specific historical setting.</p><p><br /></p>
<div><strong><span style="font-size: large;">Absence of Mass Proselytism</span></strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<div></div>
<p>- <b>A Messenger’s operational duty in the Quran is solely to convey information clearly (balagh) to their own people (qawm) only</b>. Rooted strictly within the Abrahamic ancestral line (QS. 2:132; 22:78), this mission was never a project for the forced religious expansion or conversion of outside non-Arab ('Ajam) nations. This practical mission concluded entirely upon his passing as the Seal of the Prophets (خاتم النبيين - QS. 33:40).</p><p><br /></p>
<h2><strong>3. The Theological Concept of "Muslim" Status (QS. 22:78)</strong></h2><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<div></div>
<div></div>
<div><strong><span style="font-size: large;">Identity by Divine Designation and Lineage</span></strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<div></div>
<p>- <b>The status and title of "Muslim" is a specific designation chosen and bestowed directly by God upon the immediate followers who struggled alongside the Prophet</b>. This identity is literally anchored in biological ancestry through the phrase "millata abikum" (the creed of your forefather Abraham), denoting actual genealogical descent rather than a mere "spiritual fatherhood" extended to outsiders.</p><p><br /></p>
<div><strong><span style="font-size: large;">Not a Voluntary Human Claim</span></strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<div></div>
<p>- <b>This refutes the modern romanticism of religious conversion or "reverting</b>." In the context of the text, becoming a Muslim is an absolute prerogative of <b>divine genealogical selection</b>, rather than a matter of free human choice in later generations.</p><p><br /></p>
<div><strong><span style="font-size: large;">The Meaning of Min Qablu (مِنْ قَبْلُ)</span></strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<div></div>
<p>- <b>Translating to "before this" or "from the past," this phrase asserts that the naming and isolation of this chosen group's identity was already anchored in God's selection of historical chosen ones (QS. 3:33) and the explicit designation of their descendants as Muslimun (QS. 2:132).&nbsp;</b>Rather than denoting a metaphysical or spiritual mysticism, this ancestral <b>selection</b> was an empirical, genealogical reality set in place long before the community itself came into physical existence.</p><p><br /></p>
<h2><strong>4. Implications for Modern-Day Sharia Law</strong></h2><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<div></div>
<div></div>
<div><strong><span style="font-size: large;">Laws Limited to the Chosen Lineage's Contemporary Followers</span></strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<div></div>
<p>- <b>Technical and sociocultural rules,&nbsp;commands, and prohibitions—known as prescriptive rulings (al-ahkam al-taklifiyyah)—strictly bound the specific group of Al-Nas (الناس) rooted in this divine genealogical selection</b>. These obligations applied exclusively to The Abrahamic descendants who pledged allegiance (bay'ah) to the Prophet during his lifetime, rather than serving as a universal legal code for outside nations (non-descendant populations today).</p><p><br /></p>
<div><strong><span style="font-size: large;">Cross-Cultural Flexibility</span></strong></div><div><strong><br /></strong></div>
<div></div>
<p>- <b>Modern non-Arab ('Ajam), non-descendant populations bear no theological obligation to adopt the localized legal culture of 7th-century Arabia</b>. What the outside world <b>draws from it</b> instead is the universal substance of Quranic values—such as justice, morality, and humanity—to be applied organically within their respective local civilizations.</p><p><br /></p><h2 style="text-align: left;">Conclusion&nbsp;</h2><p>Separating universal human values from localized ancestral <b>mandates</b> brings clarity to how the Quran engages with both its immediate historical audience and the wider world. While the scripture and its legal obligations belong strictly to the chosen Abrahamic <b>legacy</b>, its timeless principles of justice, morality, and human dignity remain <b>accessible to all</b> civilizations today.&nbsp;</p>
